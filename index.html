First law of thermodynamics (Week 2)

Here are some comments on the material in Thompkins Chapter 1

1Conservation of energy¶

Adrian Thompkins (eq. 1.9) writes the first law as:

du=đq−đwdu = \dbar q - \dbar wdu=đq−đw
(1)

(although the bar notation isn’t used by AT, it’s more informative, since the đ\dbarđ symbol indicates that only the variable uuu can actually have a differential. To understand why, it helps to think of the internal energy, which is a state variable (i.e. and instantaneous function of the system) as a noun and heating and working as verbs. You can’t take derivative of heating and working because they are processes, not variables. You can, however, ask how much energy heating and working contribute to the system over a very short time interval, and write that contributions as đq=qtdt\dbar q = q_t dtđq=qt​dt and đw=wtdt\dbar w = w_t dtđw=wt​dt, where qtq_tqt​ and wtw_twt​ are the heating and working rates, with units of J kg−1s−1\un{J\,kg^{-1} s^{-1}}Jkg−1s−1.

When in doubt, you can do any thermodynamic derivation in terms of derivative instead of differentials, and write:

dudt=qt−wt\frac{du}{dt} = q_t - w_tdtdu​=qt​−wt​
(2)

or alternatively:

du=qtdt−wtdtdu = q_t dt - w_t dtdu=qt​dt−wt​dt
(3)

Note that qtq_tqt​ and wtw_twt​ have the units of a rate (J/kg/s), and are functions. Below, I’ll use differentials to derive cvc_vcv​ (see eq. (8)) and derivatives to derive cpc_pcp​ (equation (16) following Bohren and Albecht), so you can see the difference in approach.

Some definitions

qtq_tqt​ is the rate of change of internal energy of the system as a consequence of a temperature difference between it and its surroundings

and wt (J kg s−1)w_t\ (\un{J\,kg\,s^{-1}})wt​ (Jkgs−1) is the working rate:

rate of change of internal energy of the system as a consequence of of a force exerted through a distance (positive if work done by the system

and in another text, (Q (J)Q\ (\un{J})Q (J)) similarly is defined as:

... any spontaneous flow of energy from one object to another caused by a difference in temperature between objects

Again note the use of flow -- this is a process, not a quantity.

and work is also defined as a process or flow (W (J)W\, (\un{J})W(J)):

... any other transfer of energy into or out of the system. You do work on a system whenever you push on a piston, stir a cup of coffee, or run current through a resistor.

Spoiler alert: If the above seems a little fuzzy, you can look up the statistical mechanics definition of heat and work. Bottom line is that heat is energy transfer between systems with no change to their energy levels (just changes to the number distribution of particles among those energy levels), where work involves some external change to the systems that does affect the value of the energy levels available to the the system.

2.1First pass: deriving cvc_vcv​ using differentials¶

The constant cvc_vcv​ in (5) is is the heat capacity at constant volume. To see how this is related to the first law and the state variables ρ\rhoρ, T and p, write the work done in time dtdtdt as wdt=pdvw dt = p dvwdt=pdv, where vvv is the specific volume (i.e. 1/ρ1/\rho1/ρ), then (3) becomes

3enthalpy¶

It’s not often that we get to work with constant volume situations in the atmosphere so uuu is not conserved even when q=0q = 0q=0. Also the specific volume vvv is comparatively hard to measure. More useful than the internal energy in atmospheric thermodynamics is the specific enthalpy, h (J kg−1)h\ (\un{J\,kg^{-1}})h (Jkg−1), defined as:

h=u+pvh = u + pvh=u+pv
(12)

Note that the pvp vpv term in (12) looks like pressure-volume work, and can be interpreted as the work required at constant pressure ppp to make room for the system with internal energy uuu (Schroeder p. 149).

Using hhh we can rewrite the first law (du/dt=qt−p dv/dtdu/dt = q_t - p\, dv/dtdu/dt=qt​−pdv/dt) as

dhdt=qt+vdpdt\frac{dh}{dt} = q_t + v \frac{dp}{dt}dtdh​=qt​+vdtdp​

This is Thompkins eq. 1.25

Taking dp/dt=0dp/dt = 0dp/dt=0 in (13), we can say that the heating rate qtq_tqt​ is equal to the time rate of change of enthalpy at constant pressure. Since evaporation and condensation occur at constant pressure we will find hhh useful when we deal with phase change.

4Static energy and potential temperature¶

Once again, recall the hydrostatic equation:

dp=−ρgdzdp = -\rho g dzdp=−ρgdz
(29)

Using (29) we can go back to the first law and show why enthalpy is a very useful quantity, especially to atmospheric modelers. Inserting (29) into the last line of (26) gives:

qtdt=dh+gdzq_t dt = dh + g dzqt​dt=dh+gdz
(30)

If there is no heating (i.e. the process is adiabatic) then q=0q = 0q=0 and we can integrate (30) to get a conservation equation for the dry static energy hdh_dhd​:

sd=hdry air+gz=constants_d = h_{dry\ air} + gz = \text{constant}sd​=hdry air​+gz=constant
(31)

(compare Thompkins eq. 1.43, p. 13). This conservation makes hdh_dhd​ a useful tracer, since changes to hdh_dhd​ have come come from some diabatic process (radiation, evaporation), and can’t come from simply lifting or sinking air.

Note also that gzgzgz is just the potential energy per kilogram. So as the parcel decreases or increases its altitude, energy is moving between potential and internal, while hdh_dhd​ is staying the same.
